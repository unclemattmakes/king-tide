Making of Hoverbike
Play the game Source

An open build log

How we built an arcade ocean you can race.

Hoverbike is a web-first arcade hover-bike racer — Three.js, WebGPU, and a WASM physics engine running in a browser tab. This is the making-of: each chapter takes one system, explains it in plain language, and hands you an interactive 3D demo wired to the actual game code so you can poke at it yourself.

Most "how it works" write-ups stop at a diagram. We wanted the demos to be honest, so every interactive panel here imports the same modules the shipping game runs. When you drag a slider on the wave page, you are editing the exact sum-of-sines field the bikes float on — not a lookalike rebuilt for the article.

Start anywhere. The chapters are independent, but Wave Mastery is the heart of the game, so it's the natural first read.

CHAPTER 01

Wave Mastery

The signature mechanic. How a handful of stacked sine waves becomes an ocean you can read, ride, and launch off — and why the same math runs the physics and the visuals.

CHAPTER 02

Hover & Buoyancy

Four probes, differential heights, and how a bike settles onto moving water without chattering — plus the speed trick that makes it anticipate the next wave.

CHAPTER 03

Tuning the Feel

From a twitchy prototype to a ride that feels right: the auto-leveling we deleted, the dive-then-level kick, and the tuck sweet-spot — feel as a curve, not a button.

CHAPTER 04

The Drift

Mario Kart's mini-turbo, ported to a hoverbike: why land needed its own skill, the charge tiers, and the anti-snake floor — with a charge-meter you can hold.

CHAPTER 05

Sim vs. Render

The one rule that keeps the engine honest — the sim can't see Three.js — and the freedom it buys: determinism, replays, headless tests, and two clocks.
