# Track Flow Analysis — Genre Reference Set

Cross-game deep dive on the **shape** and **beat structure** of the most
loved tracks in the four titles closest to Hoverbike's DNA: Mario Kart
8 / 8 Deluxe, Mario Kart World, Wave Race 64 (+ Blue Storm), Jet Moto
1 / 2, and the two Sonic kart racers (Transformed and CrossWorlds).

Complements the per-game files in this folder, which cover lap times,
mechanics, and reception at the **game** level. This file is at the
**track** level — what specific shapes the genre's hall-of-fame tracks
take, and what moments inside them make them rewatchable.

Intended use: a starting palette for the v1 track set in
[docs/track-themes.md](../docs/track-themes.md). When a Hoverbike
track is being blocked out, the right reference here is usually one
specific track from one specific game, not "Mario Kart" or "Wave Race"
in the abstract.

## 1. What we mean by "flow"

A track has three layers that have to compose, and "flow" is what we
call the composition working.

1. **Topology** — the graph shape. Oval, figure-8, point-to-point,
   dumbbell (Jet Moto Suicide), branching/routed, transformation-gated
   multi-modal, single-lap descent.
2. **Macro shape** — the *envelope* you trace through space. Where
   it goes vertically. Where it opens up and where it pinches. The
   ratio of straights to turns. Whether the third lap looks different
   from the first.
3. **Beat structure** — the rhythm inside the lap. Most loved tracks
   read as 3–5 distinct "sections" of 8–20 s each, with one named
   set-piece that the rest of the lap is paced around.

A track without topology is a tech demo. A track without macro shape
is a memory test. A track without beat structure is a hot lap, not
a race. The genre's best tracks nail all three.

## 2. Topology taxonomy

The eight shapes that account for >95% of memorable tracks in the
reference set, in rough order from most common to least.

| # | Shape | Examples | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Loop** (rounded rectangle / kidney) | Sunny Beach, Hammerhead, Joyride, most MK nitros | Easy to read, lap memorable after 1 race | Generic if signature moment is absent |
| 2 | **Figure-8 with crossover** | GCN Yoshi Circuit, Bingo Highway, Cypress Run | Built-in re-entry to the same set-piece from two angles | Crossover bridge limits vertical play |
| 3 | **Branching / routed** | Acorn Heights, Crown City, Coral Town, Marine Fortress shortcut | Replayability per-lap; rewards repeat play | Hard to balance "fastest route" without a meta forming |
| 4 | **Single-lap point-to-point descent** | Mount Wario, MKW Wilderness routes | Cinematic, no repetition fatigue, naturally escalates | Once-and-done; doesn't reward memorization |
| 5 | **Dumbbell / Suicide** | Suicide Swamp, MK Wuhu Loop variants | Head-on traffic = constant chaos | Confusing for new players; AI hates it |
| 6 | **Transformation-gated multi-modal** | Ocean View, Adder's Lair, Rogue's Landing | Per-lap escalation built in (different vehicle each lap) | Heavy tech lift; can feel like 3 mini-tracks |
| 7 | **Vertical column / spiral** | Shy Guy Falls, Dragon Driftway, Cloudtop Cruise | Verticality reads from any camera; "how do I get up there" hook | Camera fights you on the cant changes |
| 8 | **Möbius / inverted loop** | MK8 Mario Circuit, Electrodrome ribbons | One-of-one wow factor first time | Only works once per game; gimmick wears off |

The genre clusters around shapes 1–4. Shapes 5–8 are the headline
"this is the one you remember" tracks that ship one or two to a game,
not eight.

## 3. The recurring features of beloved tracks

Patterns that show up in nearly every fan-favorite track across all
four games, regardless of topology:

- **One named set-piece per track.** Mount Wario's ski jump. Big
  Blue's mag-strip wall. Cloudtop Cruise's beanstalk. Ocean View's
  orca. Wave Race 64 Sunny Beach's wraparound ramp. Without a named
  moment, the track is "the one with the trees."
- **The set-piece is on lap 1.** Players need it inside the first
  90 seconds or it doesn't enter the memory of someone who plays the
  cup once and stops. Beloved tracks front-load.
- **A "recovery straight" right after the hardest section.** Drake
  Lake's mid-course open water after the post forest; Mount Wario's
  cave exit after the slalom; Adder's Lair's lava-river opening
  after the volcano-collapse turn. The hard part needs an exhale.
- **Verticality changes the line.** Tracks remembered as "great" are
  almost always non-planar: real elevation change, real wall-cant,
  real drops. Flat ovals are forgotten.
- **At least one bisected path.** Loved tracks always offer a "go
  left for the safe line, right for the risky shortcut" choice at
  least once. Pure single-line tracks read as autopilot.
- **The finish line is visible from somewhere before it.** Mount
  Wario's final ski-jump shows you the lodge. Sunny Beach loops you
  past the start/finish at the wraparound. Telegraphing the finish
  is what makes the final 5 seconds feel like a finish.
- **Music drop synced to the set-piece.** MK8 Cloudtop Cruise is the
  canonical example — the music change as you exit the storm cloud
  is widely cited as the moment players remember. Soundtrack is part
  of the flow, not over it.

## 4. Per-game deep dives

Each entry: **Topology · Macro shape · Section beats · Signature
moment · Why it works · Hoverbike takeaway.**

### 4.1 Mario Kart 8 / 8 Deluxe

The most-cited fan-favorites in community polls and critic lists are
Mount Wario, Big Blue, Electrodrome, Mario Circuit (the Möbius one),
Cloudtop Cruise, Dragon Driftway, Shy Guy Falls, and the N64 Rainbow
Road remake.

#### Mount Wario

- **Topology:** Single-lap point-to-point descent.
- **Macro shape:** Vertical drop from peak to base. ~1,300 m of
  altitude lost across three named sections. Single direction, no
  repetition.
- **Section beats:** (1) cargo-plane drop onto icy summit, dive into
  cave; (2) anti-grav dam wall + woodland slalom; (3) ski-cross with
  moguls and gates → ski-jump → final S-curve into lodge.
- **Signature moment:** The ski-jump anti-grav pad into the gliding
  finish. Visible lodge at the end of the trajectory.
- **Why it works:** Eliminates the lap-repetition problem entirely.
  Each segment is its own genre (cave / dam / ski course). The
  "easily understandable trajectory" (we're going down a mountain)
  lets the game escalate without losing readability.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** A point-to-point "drowned city descent"
  track — start above the waterline on a rooftop, end at sea level
  through three flooded districts. Already noted in
  [docs/track-themes.md](../docs/track-themes.md) as the SF-flood
  candidate. Mount Wario is the explicit reference.

#### Big Blue

- **Topology:** 3-section single-lap, F-Zero ribbon.
- **Macro shape:** Long sweeping curves with steep wall-cant
  (anti-grav), interspersed with high-speed straights. Sits over an
  ocean canyon.
- **Section beats:** (1) high-speed straight + first cant; (2) S-curve
  ribbon with wall-riding; (3) drop section + finish straight.
- **Signature moment:** The first time you go up the wall — the
  camera holds horizontal while the world rotates.
- **Why it works:** Honors the F-Zero source while staying in MK's
  speed band. Sells anti-grav as a feature, not a gimmick.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Bank-cant on wave faces is our anti-grav.
  A "Big Blue" pass would be a long open-water straight with banked
  wave-walls you ride at full pump — the wave physics already
  supports it, the camera doesn't yet.

#### Electrodrome

- **Topology:** Loop with vertical figure-8 over a central arena.
- **Macro shape:** Indoor neon stadium; the loop crosses itself
  vertically. Anti-grav stripes on the upper deck.
- **Section beats:** (1) starting straight + first anti-grav lift;
  (2) upper-deck ribbons with wall riding; (3) glide drop back to
  ground floor; (4) final tight turn under the bridge.
- **Signature moment:** The "drop the beat" anti-grav transition with
  the music swelling — track and soundtrack are locked together.
- **Why it works:** Music-as-mechanic. Sells the anti-grav system
  on every lap by varying which surfaces are wall-rides.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Tie wave-pump-state to an audio mix layer.
  Pumping correctly = music brightens. This is cheap and gives the
  hero mechanic an extra channel of feedback.

#### Mario Circuit (Möbius)

- **Topology:** Möbius strip loop — the track twists 180° so the
  "ceiling" becomes the "floor" once per lap.
- **Signature moment:** Realizing the loop's inside is the outside.
  Universally cited as MK8's single best use of anti-grav.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** One-of-one. We probably don't ship a
  Möbius track but we should ship *one* "you have to see it to
  believe it" geometric trick — that's the slot it occupies in the
  player's memory of the game.

#### Cloudtop Cruise

- **Topology:** Loop, vertically stratified.
- **Macro shape:** Starts on a beanstalk launching above clouds;
  middle section flies between Bowser airships through a lightning
  storm; descends back to the cloud floor.
- **Section beats:** (1) beanstalk climb + Bowser-balloon bounce
  pad; (2) airship-deck crossings with cannon obstacles; (3) storm
  glide; (4) cloud-floor finish.
- **Signature moment:** The storm — sky darkens, lightning flashes,
  the music shifts to a major-key brass swell. The track audibly
  *exhales* on exit.
- **Why it works:** Weather-as-section-break. The player feels the
  middle of the track as a *different place* even though it's the
  same loop.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** We already have storm/weather hooks in the
  wave system. One v1 track must use a weather window as a
  beat-structure tool: rough water for 15 s, then calm for the
  finish. Lights, music, particle density all shift.

#### Dragon Driftway

- **Topology:** Loop draped around a giant serpent.
- **Macro shape:** ~95% anti-grav. You drive *on* the dragon's
  back; the curves come from the creature's body shape.
- **Signature moment:** Going into the dragon's mouth at the start.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Track-as-creature is hard to justify in
  the drowned-cities vision but the principle (the *environment*
  is the track, not just its setting) transfers. A flooded football
  stadium where the seating bowl is the cant works the same way.

#### Shy Guy Falls

- **Topology:** Loop with vertical column.
- **Macro shape:** Anti-grav up the inside of a waterfall, gliding
  jump across the top, descent down the outside back to start.
- **Signature moment:** Driving up the waterfall.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Wave-pumping up a *vertical* wave wall is
  the natural Hoverbike equivalent. Tidal bore or surge wave that
  carries you up the side of a building, hop the crest, ride the
  other side down.

#### N64 Rainbow Road (MK8 remake)

- **Topology:** Loop, but in MK8 the 3-lap N64 original became a
  single ~1,800 m one-lapper to compress the original's bloat.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** The single-lap-with-named-segments
  structure scales up; not every track has to be 3 × 50 s. Allow
  one or two "one-lap epics" of 2–3 minutes in the v1 set. The
  Mount Wario + Rainbow Road structure is a permission slip to
  break the 3-lap rule.

### 4.2 Mario Kart World

MK World is recent (2025) and the conversation is still settling.
The new-track community consensus highlights Great ? Block Ruins,
Acorn Heights, Cheep Cheep Falls, Faraway Oasis, Whistlestop Summit,
and Crown City. The "intermission route" tracks (long open-world
segments between named courses) are the most-criticized feature
and a useful anti-pattern.

#### Great ? Block Ruins

- **Topology:** Loop with heavy branching; final course of Banana Cup.
- **Macro shape:** Sky ruins with a colossal ? Block as the central
  landmark. Multiple discreet shortcut layers.
- **Signature feature:** Density of optional skill shortcuts —
  "filled to the brim with discreet shortcuts for pros to perform
  crazy stunts off." Pro and casual lines diverge sharply.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** The skill ceiling of a track is set by the
  *number of optional shortcuts*, not the difficulty of the main
  line. One v1 track should have 4+ shortcut paths only top players
  find.

#### Acorn Heights

- **Topology:** Branching, multi-path.
- **Macro shape:** Village + pond + climb up a colossal tree.
- **Signature feature:** "Tons of split paths that make every lap
  feel unique." Beloved for replay value, not for any single moment.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** This is the model for one of the "explorer"
  flooded-cities tracks — the layout puzzle *is* the appeal. Less
  reliance on a single hero set-piece.

#### Crown City

- **Topology:** **Three different layouts** depending on which route
  you enter from. Combined into a 3-section course for VS / TT.
- **Macro shape:** San Francisco analog (Golden Gate, SoMa,
  Embarcadero references).
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** The "drowned SF" v1 track should follow
  this format directly. Different cup entries through different
  districts; a TT/VS layout that strings them all together.
  Crown City is the explicit precedent.

#### Whistlestop Summit

- **Topology:** Loop, but with **three distinct entry points** from
  the overworld; the first 5–10 s of the track differs depending
  on which route you took to get there.
- **Macro shape:** Mesa mountain themed as a steam locomotive.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Even without an overworld, the "different
  entry, same track" principle gives one track multiple flavours
  cheaply. A track variant pack where the same core loop has three
  intros saves 60% of the authoring cost of three new tracks.

#### Intermission routes (anti-pattern)

The connecting "drive from one named track to the next" segments
are the single biggest MK World complaint. Players want discrete
races. Hoverbike already locked "no intermission routes" in
[docs/design-targets.md §5](../docs/design-targets.md) — MK World
is the proof.

### 4.3 Wave Race 64 (+ Blue Storm)

Eight courses total in WR64, all open-water but with sharply
different terrain, weather, and tightness. The genre's primary
reference for "wave mastery as the skill."

#### Sunny Beach

- **Topology:** Simple loop (rounded rectangle).
- **Macro shape:** Tropical bay. Light chop. Open water + a short
  beach traverse + a wraparound through inlets.
- **Section beats:** (1) start straight across open chop; (2)
  beach wrap with sand dolphin jump; (3) inlet slalom; (4)
  finish straight past the pier.
- **Signature moment:** The first wave you take into the air after
  the start — the game's first lesson in wave-pumping is the first
  10 seconds of the first course.
- **Why it works:** It teaches the hero mechanic without a tutorial.
  Wave-Race 64 ships its mechanic as level 1.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** **Lagoon must teach wave pumping in its
  opening 8 seconds.** This is the explicit reason Wave Race's
  signature feels native and Riptide's feels bolted-on.

#### Sunset Bay

- **Topology:** Loop, complex.
- **Macro shape:** Harbor with multiple ramp piers crossing the
  course at angles.
- **Signature feature:** First introduction to ramps as a mechanic;
  buoy lines force major weaving (and on Hard, spike mines fill
  the gaps).
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Difficulty-tier hazard placement on the
  *same track* is cheap content depth. Hard mode shouldn't be
  "faster AI" — it should be more spike-mine equivalents on the
  pumping line.

#### Drake Lake

- **Topology:** Loop, "basically a big long square."
- **Macro shape:** Foggy lake. **Calmest water** of any course.
  Weeds that hinder speed. A dangerous wooden-post slalom in the
  back stretch.
- **Signature feature:** The mist clearing as the race progresses
  — visibility is itself a mechanic.
- **Why it works:** Inverts the genre's expectations. The
  *quietest* water is the hardest course because the wave isn't
  doing the work; you are. Teaches that wave-mastery is also
  knowing when there's *no* wave to pump.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Ship **one calm-water track** in the v1
  set. It will read as the "skill check" track where pumping
  doesn't carry you. This is what makes pumping *legible* on the
  other ten tracks.

#### Marine Fortress

- **Topology:** Loop with **branching shortcut**.
- **Macro shape:** Stormy water around a fortress complex.
  Scattered floating crates as hazards. A curvy tunnel shortcut
  that opens on Hard/Expert — hitting a wall in there is instant
  fail.
- **Signature feature:** The high-risk tunnel shortcut. Saves time
  but turns one section into a survival run.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Tunnels with one-shot-kill walls (or
  bike-totals) are a strong skill expression slot we don't yet
  use. One v1 track should have an "expert line" that's faster
  by 4–6 seconds and unforgiving.

#### Twilight City

- **Topology:** Loop, dense and twisty.
- **Macro shape:** Night-time canal racing through a city
  (the OG drowned-city track). Thin pier ramps bypass turns.
- **Signature feature:** Multiple skip-ramps you can choose to
  hit or roll past. Risk/reward per lap.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Direct reference for **any urban flood
  track**. Twilight City is the canonical "race through a city at
  water level" prior art. Riff on it; don't avoid it.

#### Glacier Coast / Southern Island

- **Topology:** Long open-water loops, often called Wave Race's
  most "open" tracks.
- **Macro shape:** Big horizontal spread, low density of set
  pieces, high reliance on wave-reading.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** The "ocean stretch" v1 track in
  [docs/design-targets.md §4 P0-2](../docs/design-targets.md)
  should reference Glacier Coast, not Sunny Beach. Glacier Coast
  is where pumping *is* the track.

### 4.4 Jet Moto 1 / 2

The genre's primary reference for **multi-surface terrain** within
a single race, and the inventor of the **Suicide course**.

#### Suicide Swamp (and the Suicide format generally)

- **Topology:** **Dumbbell.** Start grid in the middle, checkpoint
  at each end, race back and forth — usually 3 laps means 6
  traversals with constant head-on traffic.
- **Macro shape:** Swamp with mud, water, half-submerged ruins.
  Mixed terrain on both legs.
- **Signature feature:** The head-on bike weave. Once the field
  splits across both ends, you're closing on opponents at relative
  speed for the entire race.
- **Why it works:** Solves the "lonely race" problem that plagues
  fast tracks. The pack is *always* visible because half of it is
  coming at you.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Worth a single experiment for v1.x. Pair
  it with the "Baby-Park-style chaos arena" slot from
  [design-targets §4 P0-2](../docs/design-targets.md). A
  flood-canal dumbbell where boats pass each other on both legs
  could be the chaos track instead of a multi-lap oval.

#### Cliffdiver

- **Topology:** Loop, dramatic vertical.
- **Macro shape:** Long dirt trail past Gem Bay → Switchbacks
  section with sharp windy turns + magnetic poles (the grapple
  mechanic) → Lookout → **cliff drop over rocks and water into
  the finish**.
- **Signature moment:** The cliff drop. Universally cited as the
  series' iconic frame.
- **Why it works:** End-of-lap drop is a built-in finishing
  flourish. Lap 3 ending on a cliff dive reads as a *finish*, not
  a finish line.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** **Mandatory.** At least one v1 track
  ends every lap with a major drop. The cliff-mesa moment from
  the current Cliffside track is right; lean further into it. The
  *last 5 seconds of a lap* are what the player remembers about
  whether they want to play another race.

#### Hammerhead

- **Topology:** Short over-water loop.
- **Macro shape:** Collapsed freeway over water, sand dunes and
  sand bars on the edges. Easiest track in JM2.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** The "ruined infrastructure" texture —
  collapsed-freeway-as-track — is the directly applicable trope
  for drowned-cities. We can use this geometry verbatim and it
  will read as Jet Moto without being derivative.

#### Joyride / Cypress Run

- **Topology:** Standard loops.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** These are the "amateur tier" tracks —
  short, gentle, used to introduce mechanics. Lagoon is our
  current equivalent and serves the same purpose.

#### Magnetic poles (cross-track mechanic)

JM's signature is the grapple-pole, used to cling to switchback
corners at full speed. Hoverbike doesn't have an equivalent and
shouldn't — wave-pumping is our signature axis. But the design
*role* the pole plays (a skill expression on tight turns that
casual players can ignore) is worth keeping in mind: do we have
an analogous expert-only line technique on Hoverbike's tight
turns? Currently no. Worth flagging as an open question.

### 4.5 Sonic & All-Stars Racing Transformed

The genre's primary reference for **transformation-gated multi-modal
tracks** — the same track changes vehicle type per lap.

#### Ocean View

- **Topology:** 3-lap loop, **vehicle changes per lap**. Lap 1 is
  car, Lap 2 mixed, Lap 3 is boat.
- **Macro shape:** Coastal road that progressively floods as the
  race goes on; by lap 3 the road is gone and you're at sea.
- **Section beats:** (1) coastal road; (2) cliff path under
  Sonic Heroes architecture; (3) ocean traversal past hazards
  (orca, robotic squid, Giant Chopper).
- **Signature moment:** The orca surfacing under you on the
  ocean lap. Cribbed from Sonic Adventure intro; works because
  it's a *jump scare in a racing game*.
- **Why it works:** Per-lap escalation built into the topology.
  Lap 3 isn't "lap 1 with smarter AI" — it's *physically a
  different course*.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** We have a free version of this — the
  **tide rises** between laps. Lap 1 at low tide exposes a path
  through ruins; lap 3 at high tide that path is submerged and
  you take an aerial alternative. Cheap, on-brand, ties to
  weather system. Strong candidate set-piece for one v1 track.

#### Adder's Lair

- **Topology:** Loop with **destructible layout**.
- **Macro shape:** Demonic lava valley. Parts of the track
  collapse during each lap as the volcano erupts. Lap 1 path ≠
  lap 3 path.
- **Signature feature:** Track destruction as content. The course
  is literally a different shape on lap 3.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Costly to author but extremely
  rewatchable. If we ship one, it's the **flood surge** track —
  a coastal town where a tsunami arrives between laps 2 and 3
  and forces you to a higher line. Same payload as Adder's Lair
  with our themes.

#### Rogue's Landing

- **Topology:** 3-mode loop (car / boat / plane).
- **Macro shape:** Skies of Arcadia pirate harbor.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Less directly applicable — we have one
  vehicle. But the principle of "the track teaches you to read
  three different surface types in one race" maps onto our
  water/land/anti-grav split. Don't ship a track that's pure
  water *or* pure land if the player has the skill to handle both.

### 4.6 Sonic Racing CrossWorlds (2025)

Latest entry in the genre. Community-favorite new tracks:
Northstar Islands, Rainbow Garden, Coral Town, Colorful Mall,
Market Street.

#### Northstar Islands

- **Macro shape:** Multi-layered industrial track navigated with
  fans, jumps, and hydraulic press obstacles. Sharp visual
  contrast from claustrophobic turns to wide outdoor reveals.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** **Contrast as a beat.** Going from a
  pinched cave/tunnel directly into an open vista is its own
  rhythm — the camera unfolds, the music opens up. We should
  schedule at least one "tunnel → reveal" transition per v1
  track.

#### Rainbow Garden

- **Macro shape:** Floral environment with **constantly changing
  elevation**.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Continuous elevation change beats
  punctuated elevation change for "feel of speed." Cliff drops
  are events; rolling elevation is the *texture* of the lap.

#### Coral Town

- **Macro shape:** Multi-level drifting with **colored pathways**
  that split racers and lead to shortcuts.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Color-coded path splits are explicit
  affordance for branching. Less arty than the MK World "hidden
  shortcut" model but more legible for new players. Worth using
  on the tutorial-adjacent track (Lagoon).

#### Colorful Mall

- **Macro shape:** Indoor mall, dense and curvy. References the
  "mall level" trope across kart racers (Coconut Mall, Shibuya
  Downtown).
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Indoor sections are an underused beat
  in water racers. A flooded shopping arcade (echoes of Wave
  Race 64 Twilight City) ticks the box.

#### Market Street

- **Macro shape:** Tight twists across three vertical layers,
  diverging pathways.
- **Hoverbike takeaway:** Vertical stacking of the same plan
  shape (a city block raced on three levels of stories) is a
  cheap way to give a small footprint feel like three tracks.

## 5. Cross-game pattern findings

Synthesizing what shows up everywhere:

1. **Single named set-piece beats density of set-pieces.** Mount
   Wario is the iconic case but Sunny Beach (one wraparound),
   Cliffdiver (one drop), Adder's Lair (one volcano) all follow
   the rule. Players remember *one* moment per track. Two diluted
   moments score worse than one strong one.

2. **The hardest section is between 55%-75% of lap distance.** Not
   the start (need to telegraph the track), not the end (need a
   recovery + flourish into the finish line). Drake Lake post-forest,
   Mount Wario gates section, Cliffdiver switchbacks all sit here.

3. **Set-pieces are visually pre-staged.** You see Cloudtop's
   beanstalk before you climb it; Mount Wario's ski jump is visible
   from above on entry; Adder's Lair's volcano dominates the skybox.
   The track *promises* the moment before delivering it.

4. **3-section tracks dominate the loved list.** Whether explicitly
   (Mount Wario, Crown City, Big Blue) or implicitly via beat
   structure (most MK loops feel like "start → middle set-piece →
   finish straight"), three is the magic number. Two is "too short to
   matter," four is "too many to remember."

5. **One signature surface per track.** Snow / sand / lava / mud /
   ice / open water / city street. Mixed-terrain tracks (Jet Moto
   Suicide Swamp, every Sonic Transformed) are the headliner
   exception — they make *terrain change* the surface itself.

6. **Anti-grav / wall-cant is most loved when it changes the line,
   not just the camera.** MK8 Mario Circuit's Möbius is the
   peak; Dragon Driftway's 95%-anti-grav is the "too much, becomes
   neutral" cautionary tale. Use sparingly to mark a *section*, not
   continuously.

7. **Branching paths pay off across replays; named moments pay off
   on first play.** A v1 track set needs both. New players come for
   the moment; returning players stay for the variability.

8. **The finish straight has a flourish.** A ramp, a tunnel exit,
   a drop, a glide. Never just a straight into the line. Sonic's
   "drift through final corner into a transformation gate" is the
   loudest version; Mount Wario's ski jump is the elegant one.

## 6. Applying it to Hoverbike v1

Concrete mapping to the 11 v1 tracks in
[docs/track-themes.md](../docs/track-themes.md). This is a
**starting palette** — each track gets a topology slot, a
single-named-game reference, and a hero moment slot.

| Slot | Topology | Direct reference | Set-piece bucket |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tutorial / Lagoon** | Loop, simple | Wave Race **Sunny Beach** | Teach the pump in the first wave |
| **Open-ocean wave hero** | Loop, sparse | Wave Race **Glacier Coast** | The wave *is* the moment |
| **Calm-water skill check** | Loop, technical | Wave Race **Drake Lake** | Slalom past hazards in still water |
| **Drowned city descent** | Single-lap P2P | MK8 **Mount Wario** | Three districts, sea-level finish |
| **Flooded SF / multi-route** | Branching, 3 layouts | MKW **Crown City** | Per-cup entry varies the lap |
| **Urban canal night** | Twisty loop | Wave Race **Twilight City** | Pier ramps as expert lines |
| **Cliff-drop coastal** | Loop, vertical | Jet Moto **Cliffdiver** | Every lap ends with the drop |
| **Tidal surge / destructible** | Loop, mutating | Sonic Transformed **Adder's Lair** | Lap 3 path is gone |
| **Stadium / anti-grav showcase** | Möbius or vertical column | MK8 **Mario Circuit** / **Shy Guy Falls** | One geometric wow per game |
| **Chaos / dumbbell** | Dumbbell | Jet Moto **Suicide Swamp** | Head-on pack traffic |
| **Storm-band weather** | Loop, weather-gated | MK8 **Cloudtop Cruise** | Calm → storm → calm per lap |

The wave-mastery thread runs through all 11 — even the urban and
descent tracks are *flooded*, so wave reading still matters. But
each track answers the question "what's the *one* thing this
track teaches that no other track in the cup teaches?"

## 7. Open follow-ups

- **Pacing across the cup, not just per track.** A 4-track cup
  needs its *own* beat structure: easy + signature + hard +
  flourish. MK8's Special Cup (Cloudtop / Bone-Dry / Bowser's /
  Rainbow) is the textbook example. Worth a follow-up doc.
- **AI behavior per topology.** Dumbbell suicide courses break
  most racing-line AI. If we ship one, the AI work is separate.
- **Camera per topology.** Vertical column tracks (Shy Guy Falls,
  Dragon Driftway) need a camera mode the chase cam doesn't have.
  Flagged for the anti-grav milestone in
  [implementation-plan.md](../docs/implementation-plan.md).
- **A "signature moment" checklist on every v1 track ship.** No
  track ships without a named, telegraphed, lap-1-visible moment.
  Goes in the DoD convention in
  [docs/v1-work-breakdown.md](../docs/v1-work-breakdown.md).
