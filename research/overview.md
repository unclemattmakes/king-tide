# Lap Length & Player-Sentiment Research — Overview

Cross-game comparison covering Mario Kart 8 / 8 Deluxe, Mario Kart World,
Wave Race 64 / Blue Storm, and Jet Moto 1 / 2. For per-game detail see the
sibling files:

## 1. Lap length & race duration at a glance

| Game | Laps | Lap time (WR) | Lap time (casual) | Total race | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MK8 / MK8DX | 3 (Baby Park 7; some 1-lap) | ~30–40 s | ~35–45 s | ~90–150 s | Mount Wario is point-to-point ~1:40 |
| MK World — circuit | 3 | n/a | ~40–80 s | ~2–4 min | 24 racers |
| MK World — route+lap | 1 destination lap after long intermission | n/a | n/a | ~3–4 min | GP races 2–4 |
| MK World — Knockout Tour | 5–6 stitched segments | n/a | n/a | ~10 min | Headline mode |
| Wave Race 64 | 3 (adjustable 3–9) | ~24–30 s | ~40–70 s | ~2–3.5 min | 5-arrow buoy combo |
| Blue Storm | 3 (fixed) | ~17–25 s | ~30–60 s | ~2–3 min | 5-state dynamic weather |
| Jet Moto 1 | 3 | ~50–70 s | 90–120 s | 4–6 min | 20-racer grid |
| Jet Moto 2 | 3 | ~50–70 s | 90–120 s | 4–6 min | Same structure, tighter handling |

**Headline number:** every series in this set converges on a **3-lap default**
and a **~2–6 minute race**, even across 30 years and four publishers. The
outliers are deliberate identity choices: Baby Park (7 laps), Mount Wario
(point-to-point), Suicide Swamp (point-to-point with U-turn), Knockout Tour
(continuous 6-segment elimination).

The **casual lap-time band is 40–90 s**; Wave Race sits at the short end
(slalom rewards tight, fast loops) and Jet Moto at the long end (varied
terrain demands longer courses to express each surface).

## 2. Gameplay similarities

- **Three-lap arcade race as the default unit**, with at least one
  signature deviation per series (Mount Wario, Knockout Tour, Dolphin Park
  practice, Suicide point-to-point).
- **Track count usually 8–32**, padded with retro / unlock courses. Players
  always want more.
- **Risk/reward layer over base driving**: items (Mario Kart), buoys (Wave
  Race), grapple poles (Jet Moto). Each adds a "second skill axis" so the
  game isn't only about steering and throttle.
- **A signature handling feel** that defines the franchise: anti-grav
  transitions (MK8), wake-physics weight (WR64), magnetic grapple slingshot
  (Jet Moto). Every series we looked at survives or fails on whether its
  handling has a fingerprint.

## 3. Gameplay differences

- **Field size.** MK8 = 12, MK World = 24, Wave Race = 4–8, Jet Moto = 20.
  Pack size drives feel: small fields are competitive/clean, large fields
  are chaotic/cinematic. Jet Moto used 20 specifically to differentiate
  from Wipeout/Ridge Racer.
- **Surface model.** Mario Kart tracks are homogeneous (anti-grav and
  gliding are *route* options on a uniform surface); Wave Race has one
  surface (water) with extreme dynamics; Jet Moto has six surfaces in a
  single track. Hoverbike sits closer to Jet Moto's promise.
- **Item economy.** Only Mario Kart leans on it. Wave Race has zero items;
  Jet Moto has limited turbos and the grapple but no offensive items.
- **Race progression.** Mario Kart and Jet Moto are cup/series-driven. Wave
  Race is championship + stunt. MK World adds open-world traversal and
  elimination-survival (Knockout Tour) on top.

## 4. Audience

- **MK8 / MK World:** mass-market family + competitive scene at the
  margins. MK World stretches younger via art direction, older via $80
  pricing pressure and 24-player chaos.
- **Wave Race:** Nintendo enthusiasts, retro-curious, water-sim fans. Cult
  rather than mass.
- **Jet Moto:** late-90s edgy/extreme-sports demographic — surf-rock,
  sponsor branding, comic-book bios. The closest thing to our target
  audience. The franchise has been dormant for 25+ years and the
  retrospective community is small but vocal.

## 5. What players love — convergence

Across all four entries, the same praise themes recur:

- **Soundtrack.** Universally cited. MK8's live-band jazz, MK World's
  art-direction music, WR64's summery 25-track score, Jet Moto's surf-rock /
  psychobilly. *Music does heavy lifting.*
- **Environmental variety.** Mount Wario, Electrodrome, Glacier Coast,
  Marine Fortress, Hammerhead, Cliffdiver — players remember **set-piece
  courses**, not generic ovals.
- **Tactile signature feel.** Anti-grav transitions, water wake, magnetic
  grapple — the moment you feel the mechanic land.
- **Skill ceiling.** Drift snaking, buoy-combo lines, grapple-timing — each
  game's depth is in its sub-mechanic.
- **Spectacle in chaos.** MK World's 24-player Knockout, MK8 item swings,
  Jet Moto's 20-bike pack starts. Players love being *outnumbered* if the
  game still rewards play.

## 6. What players hate — convergence

- **Rubber-band / catch-up AI** is the #1 complaint in MK8, MK World, and
  Jet Moto 2 (where "ghost-recording AI" produced the same feel via a
  different bug). Wave Race largely escapes this because AI is less
  central — the buoy rule disciplines the player, not the field.
- **Items / luck dominating skill** at high pack sizes. MK World's 24
  racers amplified the existing MK8 complaint.
- **Sequel loses the feel.** Blue Storm twitchy vs WR64 weighty. JM2
  "tightened" but felt like JM1.5. MK World controversially traded
  circuit purity for traversal. Every series in this set has the
  "first game was better at the core" critique somewhere.
- **Difficulty walls.** Wave Race's DQ-on-5-misses; Jet Moto's slippery
  JM1 controls; MK8's 200cc rubber-banding.
- **Missing modes / thin single-player.** MK8 lacks Mission Mode. MK World
  has bare-bones Battle Mode. Wave Race never got a story mode. Jet Moto
  has placement series and stunt; nothing structured.
- **Pricing & repackaging.** MK8 Deluxe re-sell complaints; MK World $80
  backlash; Jet Moto's Championship Edition cut features. Players punish
  perceived value gaps loudly.

## 7. What players want — convergence

- **More tracks.** Every game. Always.
- **Course / track editor + user content.** Asked for in Mario Kart and
  Wave Race threads; implied for Jet Moto.
- **Better single-player.** Mission Mode (MK8), Adventure / story mode
  (MK8 + MK World), meaningful free-roam objectives (MK World), real AI
  (Jet Moto).
- **A new entry.** The dominant ask for Wave Race and Jet Moto. Both
  franchises are dormant and the retrospective community is unanimous.
- **Online ranked + better matchmaking** — MK8, MK World, and any Wave
  Race / Jet Moto revival wishlist.

## 8. Sentiment divergence

- **Mario Kart**'s problems are luxury problems — too few new courses, too
  many items, sequel pricing. Both games sit at Metascore 86+. The base
  game is loved.
- **Wave Race** sentiment splits between "WR64 is a masterpiece" and "Blue
  Storm misunderstood the feel." The frustration is *with Nintendo* for
  not making another one, not with the games themselves.
- **Jet Moto** sentiment is the most poignant: deep love for JM1 / JM2,
  open contempt for JM3, and grief over a cancelled JM2124 / SOLAR. The
  community explicitly says "even an indie team could do this," which is
  directly relevant to our scope.

## 9. Takeaways for Hoverbike

1. **Target a 3-lap race, 60–90 s per lap, 3–5 min total** as the
   default unit. Match Jet Moto's pacing — long enough for terrain to matter,
   short enough for arcade flow.
2. **Build one signature mechanic with clear tactile feedback** (grapple,
   buoy combo, anti-grav). Every series we looked at lives or dies on this.
3. **Lean on multi-surface physics**, the Jet Moto promise that no current
   mainstream racer delivers.
4. **Curated set-piece courses > generic loops.** Players remember Mount
   Wario, Glacier Coast, Cliffdiver — and we should ship 3–5 of those over
   12 generic tracks.
5. **Don't ship a Knockout Tour without also shipping pure 3-lap races.**
   Mario Kart World's mistake. Give the player both modes and let them
   pick.
6. **Real AI, not ghost-recordings, not rubber-band.** This is the most
   universally hated category across all four series.
7. **Track editor / mod support is the single feature that all four
   communities ask for and none of them got.** Differentiator.
8. **Soundtrack is not decoration.** Budget for it like a core system.
9. **Sequel watchout:** WR64 → Blue Storm and JM1 → JM2 both show that
   "more content + tightened controls" can still feel like a regression
   if you lose the original handling DNA. The first build's *feel* must be
   preserved through iteration.
